Gochujang: How to Use Korea's Signature Chili Paste

October 4, 2026 • 3 min read

A fermented paste of red chili, glutinous rice, and soybeans—a secret weapon for savory-spicy marinades and glazes.

Gochujang is much more than just a hot sauce. Thanks to months of fermentation, it delivers a complex flavor profile: moderate heat, deep sweetness, and rich umami.

3 Hacks for Cooking with Gochujang:

  1. Always thin it out: The paste is very thick and concentrated. Whisk it with soy sauce, rice vinegar, honey, or sesame oil.
  2. Ideal glaze: Mix gochujang with honey and garlic to brush baked wings or tofu 5 minutes before they finish cooking for a caramelized crust.
  3. Balance the richness: The paste's heat cuts nicely through rich pork dishes, fried poultry, or roasted eggplant.

Buying, Storage, and Traditional Roots

How to buy and store: Look for the paste in sealed plastic tubs or traditional earthenware crocks. Because of its natural fermentation, gochujang keeps in the fridge for months, growing darker and deeper in flavor over time.

Back home in Korea, gochujang is prized far beyond marinades: it forms the base of warming stews (such as sundubu-jjigae) and hearty braises with tofu and pork.

Stir a teaspoon of gochujang into your regular mayonnaise or sour cream for an easy, punchy sauce for burgers or fries.
